
\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}\label{sec:overview}

CERT-CD has two certificates and one rule for combining them. The
\emph{adjacency certificate} for a pair is a sequential test of the hypothesis
that some admissible conditioning set makes the partial regression coefficient
zero; it fires when the evidence against that hypothesis, minimised over
conditioning sets, is large. The \emph{orientation certificate} for a pair
compares two sequentially fitted forecasters of the joint law and fires when one
factorisation predicts strictly better. The combining rule is that the graph is
built only from fired certificates, starting from the empty graph. Each certified
edge carries a bound on the probability that a non-adjacent pair is ever
certified (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}); the bound is exact under Gaussian regression
errors and asymptotic otherwise. A certified direction is a statement about
predictive fit (Section~\ref{sec:orientation}); inside the multivariate algorithm the
orientation layer has only a conditional guarantee (Appendix~\ref{app:linear}).



\subsection{Setting and assumptions}\label{sec:setting}

Let $\Gstar$ be a directed acyclic graph on $V=\{1,\dots,d\}$ and let
$X=(X_1,\dots,X_d)$ follow a linear structural equation model
$X = B^\top X + e$, where $B$ is supported on the edges of $\Gstar$ and the
errors $e_1,\dots,e_d$ are mutually independent with finite fourth moments.
Observations $o_1,o_2,\dots$ are i.i.d.\ draws of $X$ and arrive in batches;
$\Fcal_t$ is the $\sigma$-field of the first $t$ observations. We write
$\pa(i)$ and $\de(i)$ for the parents and descendants of $i$ and
$\Scal_k(i,j)=\{S\subseteq V\setminus\{i,j\}: |S|\le k\}$ for the admissible
conditioning sets, a family fixed before any data are seen.

\begin{assumption}[Separator size]\label{ass:sep}
Every non-adjacent pair of $\Gstar$ has a d-separating set of size at most $k$.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:sep} is structural. It holds whenever $k$ is at least the
maximum in-degree of $\Gstar$, because for non-adjacent $i,j$ either
$\pa(i)$ or $\pa(j)$ d-separates them; $k=d-2$ makes it vacuous at an
exponential computational cost.

For a pair $(i,j)$ and a set $S$ let $\beta_{i\leftarrow j\cdot S}$ denote the
coefficient of $X_j$ in the population regression of $X_i$ on
$(1,X_j,X_S)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:partial}
If $S$ d-separates $i$ and $j$ in $\Gstar$, then
$\beta_{i\leftarrow j\cdot S}=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
In a linear structural equation model with independent errors the covariance of
$X$ is $\Sigma=(I-B)^{-\top}\Omega(I-B)^{-1}$ with $\Omega$ diagonal, and the
partial covariance of $X_i$ and $X_j$ given $X_S$ is a rational function of the
entries of $\Sigma$. It depends on the error laws only through $\Omega$. For
Gaussian errors, d-separation of $i$ and $j$ by $S$ implies conditional
independence by the global Markov property and hence a zero partial covariance
\citep{spirtes2000causation}; the partial covariance is the same function of
$(B,\Omega)$ for any other error laws with the same variances, so it vanishes
for them as well. The population regression coefficient $\beta_{i\leftarrow
j\cdot S}$ is proportional to the partial covariance.
\end{proof}

\noindent This is a moment condition, weaker than conditional independence, and
it does not require Gaussian errors. Faithfulness is the converse and is not
assumed.

\subsection{Certificates}\label{sec:principle}

\begin{definition}\label{def:cert}
Let $\phi$ be a feature of $\Gstar$. A \emph{certifying null} for $\phi$ is a
hypothesis $H_\phi$ that holds whenever $\phi$ is false. A process
$(E_t)_{t\ge 0}$ is \emph{Ville-valid} for $H$ if $E_t\ge 0$ and
$\Prob(\exists t: E_t\ge\lambda)\le 1/\lambda$ for every $\lambda\ge 1$ and every
$P\in H$. A \emph{certificate for $\phi$ at level $\alpha$} is the event that a
Ville-valid process for $H_\phi$ reaches $1/\alpha$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Soundness]\label{prop:single}
If $\phi$ is false, the probability that it is ever certified at level
$\alpha$ is at most $\alpha$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent The proof is the definition: if $\phi$ is false then $P\in H_\phi$.
The implication runs one way. $H_\phi$ may also hold when $\phi$ is true, and
the certificate then never fires; this is what buys validity without
faithfulness and what costs power. Three consequences follow. There is no
certifying null for ``$i$ and $j$ are non-adjacent'', so the algorithm cannot
delete and must start from the empty graph. Its output is three-valued
(certified adjacent, certified with a direction, undecided). And power is
descriptive: nothing guarantees that a true edge is ever certified.

\emph{Ville-valid} is weaker than \emph{e-process}, which requires
$\Exp[E_\tau]\le 1$ at every stopping time of a single filtration. All our
bounds use only the event that a process ever reaches a level, so they hold at
every data-dependent stopping time of the run, but we do not claim the
stronger property. Section~\ref{sec:primitive} shows why.

\subsection{The adjacency certificate}\label{sec:adjacency}

For a pair $(i,j)$ consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sepnull}
H^{\mathrm{sep}}_{ij}:\quad \exists S\in\Scal_k(i,j)\ \text{ with }\
\beta_{i\leftarrow j\cdot S}=0 .
\end{equation}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:partial} and Assumption~\ref{ass:sep}, $H^{\mathrm{sep}}_{ij}$
holds for every non-adjacent pair, so it is a certifying null for adjacency.
Let $E^{(S)}_t$ be a Ville-valid process for the point null
$\beta_{i\leftarrow j\cdot S}=0$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjacency}
A_t(i,j)=\min_{S\in\Scal_k(i,j)} E^{(S)}_t(i,j).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:adjacency}
Let $\lambda\ge 1$ and let $(i,j)$ be non-adjacent. Then
$\Prob(\exists t: A_t(i,j)\ge\lambda)\le 1/\lambda$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose $S^\star\in\Scal_k(i,j)$ with $\beta_{i\leftarrow j\cdot S^\star}=0$.
Since $A_t\le E^{(S^\star)}_t$ pointwise, the event $\{\exists t:A_t\ge\lambda\}$
is contained in $\{\exists t:E^{(S^\star)}_t\ge\lambda\}$, which has probability
at most $1/\lambda$.
\end{proof}

\noindent That the minimum of e-values is an e-value for a union null is
standard \citep{vovk2021evalues}; the proof above needs only the inclusion of
events, so the processes $E^{(S)}$ may live in different filtrations. Faithfulness enters only through power: adjacency-faithfulness makes every
$E^{(S)}$ grow for a genuinely adjacent pair, and an instance where it fails
leaves the pair undecided instead of producing a wrong edge.

\subsubsection{The per-set primitive}\label{sec:primitive}

For $S$ with $|S|=q-1$ write the regression
$X_i=\alpha^\top(1,X_S)+\beta X_j+\varepsilon$ and let $S_{ii},S_{ij},S_{jj}$
be the cross-products of the residuals of $X_i$ and $X_j$ after regression on
$(1,X_S)$, computed from the running Gram matrix of the centred data, with
$p=|S|+1$. With a right-Haar prior on the nuisance parameters and
$\beta\mid\sigma\sim N(0,\sigma^2 g)$ for a fixed $g>0$, the Bayes factor is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:safelinear}
E_n=(1+G)^{-1/2}\bigl(1-\kappa\,\hat r_n^2\bigr)^{-(n-p)/2},\qquad
G=g\,S_{jj},\quad \kappa=\tfrac{G}{1+G},\quad
\hat r_n^2=\tfrac{S_{ij}^2}{S_{ii}S_{jj}}.
\end{equation}
It depends on the data only through Schur complements of the Gram matrix, so a
single $O(d^2)$ statistic serves every hypothesis $(i,j,S)$. The prior scale
$g$ must be fixed before the data: a $g$ that adapts to the running sample size
destroys the martingale property. We fix it in advance and use a warm-up prefix,
discarded from every process, only to standardise the variables.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:safelinear}
Suppose $\varepsilon\sim N(0,\sigma^2)$ is independent of $(X_j,X_S)$ and
$\beta=0$. Then $(E_n)$ is a non-negative martingale with mean one in the
filtration generated by the sequence of maximal invariants of the nuisance group
(translations in the span of $(1,X_S)$ and rescaling of $X_i$), and therefore
$\Prob(\exists n: E_n\ge\lambda)\le 1/\lambda$ for every $\lambda\ge1$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent The proof is the right-Haar argument of
\citet{hendriksen2021optional} and \citet{grunwald2024safe} and is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:safelinear}. The regressors may have any distribution;
Gaussianity is required of the regression error $\varepsilon$, which for
$S\supseteq\pa(i)$ with no descendant of $i$ is the structural error $e_i$.

\begin{remark}[The raw-observation filtration]\label{rem:raw}
$(E_n)$ is \emph{not} a martingale in the filtration generated by the raw
observations. With $S=\emptyset$, $g=0.25$ and
$X_{1:2}=Y_{1:2}=(-1,1)$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:safelinear} gives $E_2=1$ while
$\Exp[E_3\mid\Fcal_2]=1.083>1$ under an i.i.d.\ standard Gaussian null (numerical
integration). Amenability of the nuisance group does not repair this. The
guarantee used here, a bound on the probability of ever crossing, does not need
the raw filtration, which is why Proposition~\ref{prop:adjacency} was stated for
events.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Role averaging]\label{rem:avg}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:safelinear} treats $X_i$ as the response, and the roles of $i$
and $j$ are interchangeable under the null. The implementation averages the two
role-swapped Bayes factors to remove the arbitrary choice. A crossing of the
average implies a crossing of one of the two, each of probability at most
$1/\lambda$, so the guaranteed bound for the averaged process is $2/\lambda$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Beyond Gaussian errors]\label{rem:offmodel}
When the regression error is not Gaussian, or its variance depends on the
regressors, Eq.~\eqref{eq:safelinear} is the $d=1$ case of the model-based
test of \citet{lindon2026anytime}, whose guarantee under the moment null
$E[W\varepsilon]=0$ is asymptotic and holds when the model variance equals the
heteroscedasticity-robust variance. A robust sandwich version exists
\citep[Theorem~5.4 of the arXiv version]{lindon2026anytime}. Finite-sample
validity over all finite-variance laws is impossible without tail control
\citep{shah2020hardness}. We therefore state the adjacency guarantee as
\emph{exact for Gaussian regression errors and asymptotic otherwise}, and
Section~\ref{sec:exp-primitive} measures the finite-sample departure under
heavy tails and heteroscedasticity for both versions.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[One model for both certificates]\label{rem:common}
Both certificates live in the linear structural equation model with
independent errors. Adjacency uses only second moments, through
Lemma~\ref{lem:partial}; orientation uses the shape of the error laws and, in
the additive-noise extension, nonlinear mechanisms, for identifiability only.
The validity statement of the orientation certificate
(Section~\ref{sec:orientation}) does not depend on either error law.
\end{remark}


\subsubsection{A nonparametric primitive}\label{sec:npprimitive}

The Bayes factor above needs the regression of $X_i$ on $(X_j,X_S)$ to be
linear with a constant-variance error. For the dependences that structural
equations with nonlinear mechanisms produce, this is too much to ask: if $S$
contains a mediator between $i$ and $j$ whose effect is nonlinear, a linear
regression leaves residual dependence, and the pair is certified although it is
non-adjacent (Section~\ref{sec:exp-nonlinear}). We therefore give a second
primitive, a sequential version of the generalised covariance measure of
\citet{shah2020hardness}, in the spirit of the anytime-valid conditional
independence test without the model-X assumption of
\citet{waudbysmith2023sequential}. Write
$r^{S}_{i}=X_i-\Exp[X_i\mid X_S]$. If $S$ d-separates $i$ and $j$ then
$X_i\indep X_j\mid X_S$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gcm}
\Exp\bigl[r^{S}_{i}\,r^{S}_{j}\bigr]=0 ,
\end{equation}
with no assumption on the form of the conditional means. We use three versions
of the product, $r_i^Sr_j^S$ and the two products in which one factor is the
residual of the square, $r_i^S\,(X_j^2-\Exp[X_j^2\mid X_S])$ and its transpose;
each is zero under the separation null, and the squares let the test see
dependences that are symmetric, such as $X_j=X_i^2+\epsilon$, which the plain
covariance misses.

\paragraph{Construction.} Let $\hat f^{S}_{v,s}$ be an estimate of
$\Exp[X_v\mid X_S]$ computed from the rows before row $s$ only, for $v\in\{i,j\}$
(and for the squares). The scored product is
$z_s=(X_{i,s}-\hat f^S_{i,s}(X_{S,s}))(X_{j,s}-\hat f^S_{j,s}(X_{S,s}))$. Its
running mean has the asymptotic confidence sequence of
\citet[Theorem~2.2]{waudbysmith2024asymptotic}, as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:csd}. The set
$S$ is \emph{rejected} at the first time at which the sequence for any of the three
products excludes zero (level $\alpha_{\mathrm{pair}}/3$ each), and the pair is
certified adjacent once \emph{every} $S\in\Scal_k(i,j)$ has been rejected.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:np}
Let $(i,j)$ be non-adjacent in $\Gstar$ and let $S^\star\in\Scal_k(i,j)$ separate
them. Suppose the conditional-mean estimates are predictable and the moment
conditions of \citet{waudbysmith2024asymptotic} hold for $z_s$ (finite sixth moments of $X$ suffice for the square products), and that the
product of the estimation errors, $\Exp[(f-\hat f_i)(g-\hat f_j)]$, has a running
average that is $o_P(t^{-1/2})$. Then the probability that $S^\star$ is ever rejected is at most
$\alpha_{\mathrm{pair}}$, asymptotically, and so is the probability that
the pair is ever certified.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Under~\eqref{eq:gcm} the conditional mean of $z_s$ given the past is the product
of the two estimation errors, so $z_s$ has a martingale-difference part with mean
zero and a predictable remainder; the remainder is negligible by the
product-rate condition, and the confidence sequence covers zero uniformly in $t$
with asymptotic probability at least $1-\alpha_{\mathrm{pair}}/3$ per product. A union bound over the three products gives
$\alpha_{\mathrm{pair}}$. The pair is certified only if $S^\star$ is rejected, so its
probability is smaller.
\end{proof}

\noindent The guarantee is asymptotic, and it is conditional on the estimation
error being small, uniformly enough in time that the early fits, which are poor,
do not dominate the running average of the bias. No sequential test of conditional
independence is valid uniformly over all laws \citep{shah2020hardness}, and we give
no regression class and rate for which the product-rate condition holds and do not
check it: the additive spline fit can leave a non-vanishing error product if the
conditional means are not additive in $X_S$, in which case a separating set may be
rejected and a non-adjacent pair certified. The false-edge rates measured in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} (for example $5$ of $60$ graphs at $d=16$ against a
level of $0.05$) are the evidence on this point, not a proof. In exchange the test
covers nonlinear conditional means, which the linear primitive does not.

\paragraph{Implementation.} The regression of each $X_v$ and $X_v^2$ on $X_S$ is a
ridge-penalised additive cubic spline (three knots at the quartiles of the
warm-up, inputs clipped at the $1\%$ and $99\%$ warm-up quantiles) with
the products of pairs of variables added when $|S|=2$; it is refit on all rows
seen so far when the number of rows has grown by a factor $1.25$, and the
residuals of the current fit are used for scoring the rows that follow. One
multi-output solve per $S$ serves all variables, so a pass costs
$O\bigl(\sum_{|S|\le k}n\,p_S^2\bigr)$ for the refits and $O(d^2)$ per set per
batch for scoring. For $d=8$ and $k=2$ a run on $3200$ rows takes about $0.3$
seconds, and for $d=20$ and $k=3$ (about $1350$ sets) about $25$ seconds on
$4000$ rows.

\subsection{The orientation certificate}\label{sec:orientation}

Fix a pair $(x,y)$. For each direction we fit a forecaster of the joint law on
the past and score it on the next observation. Let
$p^{x\to y}_s$ be the density of the forecaster for the factorisation
$p(x)\,p(y\mid x)$ fitted on observations $1,\dots,s-1$ and $p^{y\to x}_s$ the
mirror forecaster, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:increment}
d_s=\log p^{x\to y}_s(x_s,y_s)-\log p^{y\to x}_s(x_s,y_s).
\end{equation}
Both forecasters are $\Fcal_{s-1}$-measurable, and both are densities on the
same space, so $d_s$ is a legitimate score difference whether or not either
model is correct. Let $\bar\mu_t=t^{-1}\sum_{s\le t}\Exp[d_s\mid\Fcal_{s-1}]$
and let $\hat\mu_t$ and $\hat\sigma_t$ be the running mean and standard
deviation of $(d_s)$. The confidence sequence of
\citet[Theorem~2.2]{waudbysmith2024asymptotic} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:csd}
\hat\mu_t\pm\hat\sigma_t\sqrt{\frac{2(t\rho^2+1)}{t^2\rho^2}
\log\frac{\sqrt{t\rho^2+1}}{\alpha}},
\end{equation}
with $\rho$ tuned to a reference time $t^\ast=200$. We certify $x\to y$ at the
first $t$ at which the lower end exceeds zero and $y\to x$ when the upper end
falls below zero; both are decided only after $60$ scored observations.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:orient}
Let $\Fcal_{s-1}$ be the past, let the forecasters be $\Fcal_{s-1}$-measurable,
and suppose the martingale differences $d_s-\Exp[d_s\mid\Fcal_{s-1}]$ satisfy
the moment conditions of \citet[Theorem~2.2]{waudbysmith2024asymptotic}. Then
\eqref{eq:csd} is an asymptotic confidence sequence for $\bar\mu_t$ at level
$1-\alpha$. In particular, if $y\to x$ predicts at least as well as $x\to y$
on average ($\bar\mu_t\le 0$ for all $t$), the probability of ever certifying
$x\to y$ is at most $\alpha$ asymptotically.
\end{proposition}

\noindent What is certified is therefore that one factorisation has a
strictly better expected log score, and this is valid without either
forecaster being correct. It corresponds to the causal direction under the
identifiability results for additive-noise models
\citep{hoyer2009nonlinear,peters2014identifiability} and for linear
non-Gaussian models \citep{shimizu2006linear}: if the data come from
$x\to y$, the true class is contained in the forecaster family and the
estimates are consistent, the expected score of the correct factorisation
exceeds that of the reversed one by a positive Kullback--Leibler gap, because
for generic mechanisms or non-Gaussian errors the reversed factorisation cannot
represent the joint law. Under confounding or selection the better predictor
need not be the cause, and the certificate then says nothing about causation.
The conclusion of Proposition~\ref{prop:orient} is asymptotic, and
Section~\ref{sec:exp-pairs} reports its finite-sample behaviour.

\paragraph{Forecasters.} Both use a generalised-Gaussian density
$g(u)\propto\exp(-|u/s|^\kappa)$ with shape $\kappa\in[0.9,8]$ fitted by
maximum likelihood, a generalised-Gaussian marginal for the cause, and a
cubic truncated-power spline mean with four quantile knots, set from the
warm-up prefix, for the effect. The class \texttt{gg} has constant scale; the
class \texttt{ggh} lets the log-scale be quadratic in the cause, which covers
heteroscedastic noise
\citep{xu2022heci,immer2023loci}. The \emph{agreement} certificate reports a
direction only when both classes certify the same one. Each class has the
asymptotic guarantee of Proposition~\ref{prop:orient}, so agreement cannot, asymptotically,
certify the better-fitting direction wrongly more often than either class alone;
this concerns predictive fit and not causation, and the purpose of the rule is
protection against model-class mistakes: the
constant-scale class alone certifies the wrong direction on every
heteroscedastic Gaussian pair it decides in our simulations, and the location-scale class alone is too
flexible to resolve linear non-Gaussian pairs
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-pairs}). Preprocessing is fixed in advance: tied values
are dequantised, the order is randomly permuted, and $\max(100,\lceil0.2n\rceil)$
observations form a warm-up prefix used for standardisation and knots.


\paragraph{Fitting.} Both classes standardise $x$ and $y$ on the warm-up and set
four knots at the $20,40,60,80\%$ quantiles of the warm-up. The conditional
density of the effect given the cause is fitted by maximising the generalised
Gaussian likelihood with the mean a cubic truncated-power spline of the cause,
by iteratively reweighted least squares with a continuation over the shape
grid, so that the Gaussian shape $\kappa=2$ and the Laplace shape $\kappa=1$ are
both reachable. In class \texttt{ggh} the scale is $\exp(\gamma_0+\gamma_1x+\gamma_2x^2)$,
re-estimated by two alternating rounds of weighted refitting. The forecasters are
refit on all rows before each batch of $50$ scored rows, and each scored row
contributes one term $d_s$. A direction is reported at the first batch after
$60$ scored rows at which the confidence sequence excludes zero, and the
verdict is not revised.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Relative-fit orientation of a pair}\label{alg:relfit}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $(x_s,y_s)_{s\le n}$, level $\alpha$, model classes $\{\texttt{gg},\texttt{ggh}\}$
\State dequantise ties; permute rows at random; standardise and place knots using the first $m_0=\max(100,\lceil0.2n\rceil)$ rows
\If{$n-m_0<100$} \Return \textsc{unscorable} \EndIf
\For{each class $c$}
  \For{each batch $s\in\{m_0,m_0+50,\dots\}$}
    \State fit $p^{x\to y},p^{y\to x}$ in class $c$ on rows $1..s-1$; score the next $50$ rows, append the $d_s$
    \If{at least $60$ scored and no verdict yet} compute $\hat\mu_t\pm r_t$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:csd}; if the lower end $>0$ set verdict $x\to y$, if the upper end $<0$ set verdict $y\to x$ \EndIf
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\State \Return the common verdict if both classes agree, else \textsc{undecided}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:orient}]
The increments $d_s$ are $\Fcal_s$-measurable and the forecasters are
$\Fcal_{s-1}$-measurable, so $d_s-\Exp[d_s\mid\Fcal_{s-1}]$ is a martingale
difference sequence. Under the moment conditions, Theorem~2.2 of
\citet{waudbysmith2024asymptotic} gives a confidence sequence for the average
conditional mean $\bar\mu_t$ that holds asymptotically, uniformly in $t$. If
$\bar\mu_t\le0$ for all $t$ then crossing of the lower end above zero implies
that the interval fails to cover $\bar\mu_t$ at some $t$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What the claim does and does not say.} The proposition is a
statement about which forecaster scores better on the sequence of fitted models,
not about the data-generating process. If neither class contains the truth, the
better-scoring direction can still be the wrong causal one; the certificate is
then valid for the statement it makes and misleading as a causal claim. Its
causal reading needs identifiability, and then only if the fitted classes
approximate the truth well enough for the gap in expected log score to have the
right sign. The agreement rule reduces, but does not remove, this risk, and
Section~\ref{sec:exp-pairs} measures it in simulation.

\subsection{The algorithm and its guarantee}\label{sec:algorithm}


CERT-CD maintains a three-valued state for every pair. It streams data in
batches, updates the running Gram matrix, recomputes $A_t(i,j)$ for every
undecided pair, and certifies adjacency when $A_t(i,j)$ crosses its threshold.
The total budget is split as $\alpha=\alpha_A+\alpha_D$, with $\alpha_A$ spread
over the $\binom d2$ adjacency nulls and $\alpha_D$ over the direction nulls,
and the thresholds are $\lambda_A=\binom d2/\alpha_A$ and
$\lambda_D=d(d-1)/\alpha_D$, fixed before the data arrive. Which pairs are
examined does not enter the bound.

\begin{theorem}[Adjacency]\label{thm:main}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:sep}, linear structural equations with independent
errors, and Gaussian regression errors in~\eqref{eq:safelinear} for every
examined $(i,j,S)$ and both roles of the pair (which holds, for example, when the
structural model is jointly Gaussian), the probability that CERT-CD ever certifies an edge between
a non-adjacent pair is at most $2\alpha_A$, uniformly over time and at every
data-dependent stopping time of the run.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Remark~\ref{rem:avg} each pair's averaged minimum has crossing probability at
most $2/\lambda_A$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:adjacency}. A union bound over at
most $\binom d2$ non-adjacent pairs gives $\binom d2\cdot 2/\lambda_A=2\alpha_A$.
\end{proof}

\noindent Choosing $\lambda_A=2\binom d2/\alpha_A$ gives $\alpha_A$ exactly; the
experiments use $\lambda_A=\binom d2/\alpha_A$, so the guaranteed bound there is
$2\alpha_A$. Under non-Gaussian errors the bound holds asymptotically
(Remark~\ref{rem:offmodel}). With the nonparametric primitive of
Section~\ref{sec:npprimitive} each pair has the level $\alpha_A/\binom d2$ and no
factor $2$ arises, so the probability that a false edge is ever certified is at
most $\alpha_A$ asymptotically, without linearity or Gaussian errors. The orientation layer inside the multivariate
algorithm uses a linear generalised-Gaussian universal-inference certificate
whose validity is conditional on the model describing each pair given a block
of conditioning variables frozen when the edge is certified
(Appendix~\ref{app:linear}); the relative-fit certificate of
Section~\ref{sec:orientation} is bivariate and is evaluated on pairs. An
extension that conditions the relative-fit forecasters on the frozen block is
possible but not evaluated here.

\subsection{Implementation of the streaming algorithm}\label{sec:implementation}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:certcd} gives the streaming procedure. The sufficient
statistic is the Gram matrix of the standardised rows, updated in $O(bd^2)$ per
batch of $b$ rows. Standardisation constants are computed once from the
warm-up prefix of $m_0$ rows, which are then discarded; after that, no quantity
used by a monitored process depends on the future.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{CERT-CD (streaming)}\label{alg:certcd}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $d$, level $\alpha$, split $\alpha_A=\alpha_D=\alpha/2$, order $k$, prior scale, batches $B_1,B_2,\dots$
\State fix $\lambda_A=\binom d2/\alpha_A$, $\lambda_D=d(d-1)/\alpha_D$; set all pairs to \textsc{undecided}
\State standardise with mean and scale of the warm-up rows; discard them
\For{each batch $B_t$}
  \State update the Gram matrix with the standardised rows of $B_t$
  \For{each undecided pair $(i,j)$}
    \State $A\gets\min_{S\in\Scal_k(i,j)}$ role-averaged $E^{(S)}$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:safelinear}
    \State record $\bar A(i,j)\gets\max(\bar A(i,j),A)$
    \If{$\bar A(i,j)\ge\lambda_A$} mark $(i,j)$ \textsc{adjacent}; freeze $Z_{ij}\gets$ certified neighbours of $i$ and $j$; open two direction processes \EndIf
  \EndFor
  \For{each open direction process $(a\to b)$}
    \State extend the numerator on $B_t$ with a forecaster fitted on rows before $B_t$ since opening
    \If{running maximum of $\log$ of the ratio $\ge\log\lambda_D$} mark $a\to b$ certified \EndIf
  \EndFor
  \State output the current marked graph (certified edges, certified arrowheads, all else undecided)
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Complexity.} Each pair has $\sum_{s\le k}\binom{d-2}{s}$ conditioning
sets, so one pass over a batch costs $O\bigl(d^2\,d^{k}\,k^3\bigr)$ for the
Schur complements, independently of $n$, and the adjacency state is $O(d^2)$.
The direction processes keep the rows since the pair was certified and refit on
them, so their cost grows with the time since certification. In our
implementation the adjacency certificates alone take about $0.1$ second for a run
on $3200$ rows at $d=8$, and the full algorithm with orientation about $53$
seconds, so the orientation processes dominate the cost
(Sections~\ref{sec:exp-scaling} and~\ref{sec:exp-ablation}). Certified pairs are skipped, and the evaluation of $A_t$ stops
as soon as one conditioning set has evidence below the threshold, since the
minimum is then known not to cross.

\paragraph{Choosing the constants.} Validity does not depend on any of the
following, and each is fixed before the monitored data arrive. The order $k$ is
the only choice with an assumption attached (Assumption~\ref{ass:sep}): any
$k$ at least the maximum in-degree suffices, and a smaller $k$ is valid as long
as every non-adjacent pair has a separator of size at most $k$; otherwise the
guarantee is lost, as the experiment on violations shows
(Section~\ref{sec:exp-scope}). The prior scale $g$ sets which effect sizes the
test is tuned to detect: with variables standardised on the warm-up, a prior
standard deviation of $0.5$ ($g=0.25$) concentrates power on coefficients of a
few tenths, and Section~\ref{sec:exp-ablation} varies it. The split of $\alpha$
between families affects the thresholds only; the batch size affects how often
the process is inspected and nothing else.

\paragraph{Properties of the output.} Because certificates are never retracted,
the output is monotone: edges and arrowheads are added and never removed, and
so the estimate at time $t$ is contained in the estimate at every later time.
Because the guarantee is time-uniform, the run may be stopped at any
data-dependent time without changing the stated bounds, and the bound does not
depend on the order in which pairs are examined. The guarantee concerns false
assertions only. It says nothing about missed edges, and the number of
undecided pairs is the price of that choice.

\paragraph{Relation to PC.} CERT-CD examines the same objects as the first
layer of PC (partial correlations given small conditioning sets) but reverses
the decision rule. PC removes $(i,j)$ as soon as some $S$ yields a
non-rejection; CERT-CD adds $(i,j)$ only when \emph{every} $S$ in the family
yields rejection, which is why the family size multiplies computation but not
the error bound: the bound for a non-adjacent pair uses only the one set that
separates it.

\paragraph{A worked example.} Figure~\ref{fig:trace} follows the adjacency
evidence for every pair in one random six-node DAG with Laplace errors (the
first draw of a fixed seed, six true edges). The running maximum of
$\log_{10}A_t$ is plotted against the number of observations. Evidence for the
true edges rises past the threshold of $\lambda_A=\binom62/0.05=300$ between
$100$ and $350$ observations and is then frozen, since certified pairs are no
longer updated; the evidence of the nine non-adjacent pairs stays below it. The
highest of these, at about $10^{2.4}$, lies just under the threshold, which is
the situation the family bound is designed to cover: with $15$ pairs, a few
non-adjacent pairs are expected to come close, and the threshold is placed so
that, in the Gaussian model, none of them crosses with probability at least
$1-2\alpha_A$.
