\title[Certificate-Only Causal Discovery]{Certificate-Only Causal Discovery:
Monitoring-Valid Edges and Predictive-Fit Orientation}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Quang-Vinh} \sur{Dang}}\email{vinh.dq4@buv.edu.vn}

\affil*[1]{\orgname{British University Vietnam}, \orgaddress{\city{Hung Yen},
\country{Vietnam}}}

\abstract{Constraint-based causal discovery deletes an edge when a
conditional-independence test fails to reject, so a lack of power is reported
as a conclusion, and its output cannot be monitored as data arrive: an analyst
who reruns it at each sample size and keeps any edge ever reported accumulates
false edges. We study certificate-only discovery, which asserts a feature of
the graph only by rejecting a null that is true whenever the feature is absent.
The adjacency certificate is the minimum over conditioning sets of sequential
tests, either a Bayes-factor test of a partial regression coefficient (exact
for Gaussian regression errors, with no guarantee we can prove otherwise) or a
nonparametric sequential generalised covariance test that allows nonlinear
mechanisms (asymptotic); it bounds the probability that a false edge is ever
certified, uniformly over time and without faithfulness, within those models. The orientation certificate compares sequentially fitted
forecasters for the two factorisations of a pair and reports a direction only
when two model classes agree; what it certifies is better predictive fit, not
causation. In 150 random graphs monitored from $n=100$ to
$6400$, CERT-CD reported a false edge in $2.7\%$ of graphs under Laplace noise (off the Gaussian model),
against $27\%$ to $91\%$ for PC, GES and DirectLiNGAM, at the price of leaving
most non-adjacent pairs undecided and a low recall at small samples. With
nonlinear mechanisms the linear test reported a false edge in about half of the
graphs and the nonparametric test in $1$ to $2$ of $60$. On the Tübingen
cause-effect pairs, under a protocol fixed before the run, the orientation
certificate committed on $12$ of $80$ scorable pairs and was right on $11$. On two physical devices with known graphs (Causal Chambers), every method,
including CERT-CD with the linear test, reported edges the graph does not
contain; with the nonparametric test and a larger conditioning order, chosen after
seeing these data, CERT-CD reported no edge outside the graph and the pairs the
graph cannot explain in $9$ and $10$ of $10$ subsamples, with recall of graph
edges of $0.12$ and $0.18$. Both certificates fail outside
their models, and we report where.}

\keywords{causal discovery, anytime-valid inference, e-values, sequential
testing, additive noise models, family-wise error control}
