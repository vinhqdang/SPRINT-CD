\section{Sampling noise}\label{sec:noise}

Table~\ref{tab:multi} gives the accuracies on T\"ubingen with exact Clopper--Pearson intervals. The half-width is about $0.10$ for every rule, and the intervals treat the pairs as independent, which they are not exactly (the official weights downweight groups of similar pairs; unweighted accuracy is used). All rules are evaluated on the same pairs, so the right comparison between two rules is the exact McNemar test on the pairs on which exactly one is correct; exact ties (a score of zero) and pairs on which a rule returned no value (TRA, $20$ pairs) are left out of the discordant counts, so TRA is compared on a different sample.

\paragraph{Two families of comparisons.} The first family has the twelve rules of Table~\ref{tab:collections2} plus our first-implementation compression with raw rows: $78$ comparisons. Twelve have a raw $p<0.05$ and two survive a Holm correction (smallest adjusted $p=0.015$); both involve the learned classifier PDM, which is below chance (PDM against faithful compression and against LOCI; $11$ of the $12$ raw-significant comparisons involve PDM), so they say nothing about the other rules. The second family fixes the ten rules that are not degenerate and not duplicated (IGCI, RECI, ANM-HSIC, pairwise LiNGAM, LOCI, ANM-GP, faithful compression, TRA, AVICI, RESIT; without PDM and without the other compression variants): $45$ comparisons, \emph{none} with a raw $p<0.05$ (smallest raw $p=0.11$, ANM-GP against RESIT). For the best rule, faithful compression ($66.7\%$), the tests against RECI, LOCI, IGCI, ANM-GP and AVICI have $p=0.39$, $0.88$, $0.14$, $0.20$ and $0.35$ (discordant pairs $20$/$14$, $23$/$21$, $29$/$18$, $24$/$15$, $32$/$24$).

\paragraph{Detectable difference.} With the observed rate of discordant pairs ($42\%$, computed on pairs where both rules have a value and neither ties; averaged over the $78$ comparisons), a simulated paired comparison on $102$ pairs reaches $80\%$ power for a difference in accuracy of $0.19$ at level $0.05$ and of $0.27$ with a Bonferroni correction over the $78$ comparisons. The simulation draws the number of discordant pairs from the observed rate, splits them with the alternative probability, uses $1000$ replications per step of $0.01$, and treats pairs as independent; the figure is approximate and applies to this discordance rate. Differences of two to five points, which separate most of the rules in recent papers, are far below it: the collection cannot resolve them, whatever the rules.

\paragraph{Graph benchmarks.} The Sachs network has 17 consensus arcs. For $22$ method configurations (Section~\ref{sec:graphs}), $16$ have a $95\%$ bootstrap interval (resampling the $55$ variable pairs) that contains the best $F_1$ ($0.67$), $7$ have a point estimate within $0.10$ of it, and of $231$ pairs of configurations $62$ differ significantly, the smallest significant difference in $F_1$ being $0.17$ (some pairs are the same method at two settings, and resampled variable pairs share nodes, so these are rough). One edge changes recall by $0.06$.
