\begin{abstract}
Methods for causal discovery are usually compared by a gain of a few accuracy points on the T\"ubingen cause--effect pairs or a few edges on a protein network. We ask what these benchmarks can resolve and why rules that test the conditional structure of a pair rarely beat much simpler rules on real data. Twelve direction rules were run under one protocol, fixed in advance, on seven real pair collections (T\"ubingen, four Causal Chambers collections of which three are new, and the Sachs arcs) and on graph benchmarks. (1) No rule leads across collections: five of twelve rules lead at least one, and the ordering of the rules on T\"ubingen is negatively correlated with the ordering on the chamber pairs (Kendall $\tau=-0.38$). (2) On $102$ T\"ubingen pairs a paired test has $80\%$ power only for a difference of $19$ accuracy points, and of $78$ pairwise comparisons two survive a Holm correction; ten defensible reporting choices move a single rule by $11$ to $26$ points and its rank by up to ten places. (3) Reproducing a published headline for a compression baseline ($74.7\%$ weighted, $68$ of $102$ pairs) required one rounding convention that the text does not give; the other plausible conventions give $68.8$ and $70.5\%$. (4) Mechanisms: a classifier that sees only the two marginals orients the chamber and Sachs pairs ($98\%$ across recordings, $87\%$ across walk recordings, $94\%$ leaving out both proteins) and transfers across collections but is at chance on T\"ubingen; additive-noise rules are near-perfect when the noise scale does not depend on the cause ($97\%$ on the least heteroscedastic third of T\"ubingen, $95\%$ in the clean cell of a factorial simulation) and reversed when it does ($29\%$ on the most heteroscedastic third, $6\%$ in simulation), shown by a matched manipulation with a marginal control. (5) Two attempts to build on this account fail with pre-registered predictions: a regime-gated rule, and a nearest-neighbour prediction of real accuracy from the simulation (Spearman $0.17$; real pairs lie two to thirteen standard deviations from the simulation's support). The account is therefore a set of demonstrated mechanisms, not a validated model. We give recommendations for reporting and an accounting of every method we tried and failed to improve, with the pre-registration log of $42$ experiments.
\end{abstract}
