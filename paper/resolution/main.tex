\begin{abstract}
Methods for causal discovery are usually compared by a gain of a few accuracy points on the T\"ubingen cause--effect pairs or a few edges on a protein network. We ask what these benchmarks can resolve and why rules that test the conditional structure of a pair rarely beat much simpler rules on real data. Twelve direction rules were run under a protocol written before the runs (departures are reported) on seven real pair collections (T\"ubingen, five Causal Chambers collections of which three are new, and the Sachs arcs) and on graph benchmarks. (1) No rule leads across collections: five of twelve rules lead at least one, and the ordering of the rules on T\"ubingen is negatively but not significantly correlated with the ordering on the i.i.d.\ and walk chamber pairs (Kendall $\tau=-0.38$ and $-0.30$, permutation $p=0.11$ and $0.21$) and positively with others. (2) On $102$ T\"ubingen pairs a paired test has $80\%$ power only for a difference of $19$ accuracy points ($27$ after correction); of $78$ pairwise comparisons two survive a Holm correction and both involve a rule below chance, and among ten non-degenerate rules none of $45$ comparisons has a raw $p<0.05$; ten reporting universes move a single rule by $11$ to $26$ points and its rank by up to ten places. (3) Reproducing a published headline for a compression baseline ($74.7\%$ weighted, $68$ of $102$ pairs) required a rounding convention that the text does not give; the other conventions give $68.8$ and $70.5\%$. (4) Mechanisms: a classifier that sees only the two marginals orients the chamber and Sachs pairs ($98\%$ from the wind to the light tunnel and back, $87\%$ across walk recordings, $94\%$ leaving out both proteins) and transfers across collections but is at chance on T\"ubingen; additive-noise rules are near-perfect when the noise scale does not depend on the cause ($97\%$ on the least heteroscedastic third of T\"ubingen, $95\%$ in the clean cell of a factorial simulation) and reversed when it does ($29\%$ and $6\%$), shown by a matched manipulation with a marginal control. (5) Two attempts to build on this account fail their pre-registered predictions: a regime-gated rule, and a nearest-neighbour prediction of real accuracy from the simulation (Spearman $0.17$). The account is a set of demonstrated mechanisms, not a validated model, and the collections beyond T\"ubingen are small (four to $47$ distinct edges). We give recommendations for reporting and an accounting of every method tried and not improved.
\end{abstract}
