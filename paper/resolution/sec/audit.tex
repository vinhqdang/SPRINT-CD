\section{A reproduction audit}\label{sec:audit}

We audited one published table in detail, because it is the one for which the protocol is specified closely enough to repeat and for which the claims concern exactly the question of this paper. \citet{stienstra2026oneruler} re-evaluate bivariate rules on the same $102$ T\"ubingen pairs with the official weights, no tuning and a decision forced on every pair. They report $74.7\%$ weighted accuracy for a parameter-free compression rule ($68$ of $102$ pairs, $66.7\%$ unweighted), $76.0\%$ for the same rule on the $100$ pairs on which SLOPE \citep{marx2017slope} is evaluated, $77.2\%$ for SLOPE under a forced decision on those pairs (from the authors' stored output) and $82.4\%$ as published (a decided-subset figure; $81.7\%$ when the pairs answered by SLOPE's significance test are scored), and $70.7\%$ for a faithful re-run of RECI; they trace the commonly quoted $77.5\%$ for RECI to a mis-copied cell. Their point is that figures obtained with test-set model selection or with abstention gated by a significance test are not comparable with forced-decision figures.

\paragraph{What we reproduce.} Our RECI re-implementation gives $70.9\%$ weighted (and $60.8\%$ unweighted) on the same pairs, within $0.2$ points of their $70.7\%$. Compression is the instructive case (Table~\ref{tab:audit}). Our first implementation followed the published description (both variables quantised to $256$ levels by linear min--max scaling, stable sort, first difference as \texttt{int8}, bzip2 level 9, normalised lengths, sign of the difference) but with the quantiser $\lfloor 256\,q\rfloor$ clipped to $255$, and with the rows capped at $2000$ and dequantised in the variant we ran at first; it gave $60.8\%$ (dequantised) and $64.7\%$ (raw rows), and $68.0\%$ and $65.9\%$ weighted. The text does not state how the integer levels are obtained from the scaled values. With $\mathrm{round}(255\,q)$, all rows (linearly thinned above $8000$) and raw values the same code gives exactly $68$ of $102$ pairs and $74.66\%$ weighted: the published figure. With $\lfloor 255\,q\rfloor$ it gives $71$ pairs and $70.5\%$. A one-pair difference in the count moves the weighted accuracy by up to six points because the official weights are unequal. Capping the rows changes the faithful variant by at most four weighted points ($71.7\%$ at $500$ rows, $74.4\%$ at $2000$, $74.7\%$ at $8000$, $75.3\%$ without thinning); the conventions and caps are in \texttt{results/exp54\_audit\_conventions.json}. We adopted the faithful convention only after the reproduction check against the published figure, and we say so: it is chosen to match a published target, not to maximise accuracy on any data of ours.

\paragraph{What we do not reproduce or compare.} We did not run SLOPE, and so cannot confirm the $77.2\%$ or $82.4\%$ figures. For LOCI, our run of the authors' code gives $64.7\%$ unweighted and $63.9\%$ weighted ($65.7\%$ and $66.4\%$ with $2000$ rows instead of $1000$); we did not locate a published T\"ubingen accuracy for LOCI that we could verify offline and make no comparison. The TRA preprint does not fix the constants we had to choose; our $64.6\%$ on the $82$ pairs it scores is for our constants and is not a test of its claims. SNOE, BF-BIC and AVICI make claims on graphs or simulated data that we did not attempt to reproduce.

\paragraph{What the audit shows for the argument.} (i) Two numbers that a reader would call ``the compression baseline on T\"ubingen'' differ by six weighted points because of an unreported rounding; a comparison between methods whose gaps are three to five points is therefore inside the variation produced by implementation conventions that no paper documents. (ii) The reproduced baseline is, on T\"ubingen, among the best rules in our panel ($66.7\%$, $74.7\%$ weighted), above RECI and LOCI but not significantly (exact McNemar $p=0.39$ and $0.88$), and it is at $38.3\%$ on the i.i.d.\ chamber pairs, where RECI is at $97.9\%$. (iii) Its authors state that the rule's signal is partly marginal-geometric (a rank transform of the inputs, which flattens marginal shape, costs sixteen points in their experiments); this is the mechanism of Section~\ref{sec:marg}.
