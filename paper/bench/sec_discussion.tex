
\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the evidence supports.} On the real data we examined, simple methods are hard
to beat. On the chamber pairs RECI, which compares two regression errors, has the highest accuracy,
not distinguishable from IGCI; on Tübingen no method is distinguishable from RECI except PDM, which is
worse. On graphs the highest $F_1$ on recordings with independent rows is DirectLiNGAM and GES. Two of
the four best-$F_1$ cells belong to methods we developed (LRG on Sachs, $0.67$, and PC with the
nonparametric test on the walk recordings, $0.42$), by margins of one or two edges and of $0.04$ that are
within sampling error. The methods we developed show their intended benefit in simulation and some
of it on measurements: lower false-edge rates under repeated inspection and nonlinear mechanisms in
simulation, and the highest precision on the chamber recordings, BTAD, at the price of recall. The
two classifiers trained on simulated data are the clearest negative results: a model that is nearly
perfect on a broad simulator can be below chance on real pairs.

\paragraph{Implications for evaluation.} First, the effective sample size of a recording
should be reported: seventeen of nineteen new chamber recordings had fewer than twenty
effective observations, which no test that assumes independent rows can cope with, and which
also contaminates reference sets built with such tests. Second, a score adjusted for
dependences the reference graph cannot explain, if used, should be shown next to the strict score,
with the construction of the adjustment disclosed and independent of the methods; ours was not
independent of the test families used by some methods. Third, the protocol matters: some of our early
results came from choices made after seeing the data, and we report which numbers are frozen and which
are not (Section~\ref{sec:provenance}).

\paragraph{Limitations.} The reference networks are small, the Sachs consensus is contested,
and the chamber graphs are bipartite, so recovery of the skeleton is mostly feature selection
from actuators to measurements. Most differences between graph methods are within sampling
error, and the effective sample size of the walk recordings limits what any method can show.
We did not evaluate amortised methods (AVICI), CAM or QCCD, and several baselines
are re-implementations or default runs (LOCI on at most $1000$ rows); a better-tuned baseline could change a ranking.
The Causal Chambers also contain interventional experiments, where a two-sample shift test recovers the
descendants of an intervened variable with no false positives on $58$ experiments ($88$ found, $20$
missed; \texttt{scripts/audit\_followups.py}), and we did not use them for the evaluation. The pair
sets are structurally easy (actuator causes with simple marginals) and the chamber pair-runs are not independent.
We did not evaluate RCC or NCC, Slope, CAM, nonlinear continuous-optimisation methods, GIES or ICP,
and we applied no multiplicity control except in the BTAD comparison. The new methods were
developed by the author of this paper, which is a source of bias; the frozen protocol and the
public code are the mitigation.
