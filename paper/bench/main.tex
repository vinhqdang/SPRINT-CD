\title[Causal discovery on real measurements]{Evaluating causal discovery methods on real measurements: cause--effect pairs, protein signalling and physical devices}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Quang-Vinh} \sur{Dang}}\email{vinh.dq4@buv.edu.vn}

\affil*[1]{\orgname{British University Vietnam}, \orgaddress{\city{Hung Yen}, \country{Vietnam}}}

\abstract{Causal discovery methods are usually validated on simulated data. We evaluate twenty-two
methods on real data with a known or consensus structure: the Tübingen cause--effect pairs, the
protein-signalling data of Sachs et al., and recordings from the Causal Chambers, physical devices
with a known graph. Twelve methods are established (PC, GES, DirectLiNGAM, NOTEARS, DAGMA and
PCMCI+ for graphs; IGCI, RECI, ANM-HSIC, a pairwise LiNGAM rule, a Gaussian-process ANM and LOCI
for pairs), four are re-implementations of 2025--2026 preprints (compression, topological residual asymmetry, BF-BIC with BOSS, SNOE-style orientation) and six were developed for this study and frozen before evaluation: sequential
certificates for edges and directions, PC with a nonparametric test, a block-replicated additive
method (BTAD) and two classifiers trained on simulated data. On the Causal Chambers pairs RECI has the
highest accuracy ($97.9\%$ and $94.3\%$ on two sets), not distinguishable from IGCI; on Tübingen
no method is distinguishable from RECI ($60.8\%$) except the classifier PDM, which is below chance
($40.2\%$, $p=0.059$) although it reaches $95\%$ on simulated pairs; all methods are
within the sampling error of chance on $17$ Sachs pairs. For graphs, the strict skeleton $F_1$ of
DirectLiNGAM and GES is highest on the chamber recordings with independent rows ($0.55$ on two
recordings, $0.70$ on two others), the methods we developed are ahead on the Sachs data
($F_1$ $0.62$ to $0.67$ against $0.58$ for PC and GES, a difference of one to two of $17$ reference
edges) and on the walk recordings ($0.42$ against $0.38$), and no method exceeds a strict $F_1$ of
$0.42$ on the seventeen random-walk recordings. Fifteen wind-tunnel recordings are random walks
with an effective sample size of about three observations out of $4000$, and a test that assumes
independent rows rejects $4.8$ times as many graph-implied independences as a block test there.
The sequential certificates keep false edges rare in simulation under repeated inspection and
nonlinear mechanisms, but not on the chambers. We state which choices were fixed before and after
seeing the data, and the limits of the evidence: few reference edges, few recordings per group,
and several methods not evaluated.}

\keywords{causal discovery, benchmark, real data, cause--effect pairs, Causal Chambers, serial dependence}
